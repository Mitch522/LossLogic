# Liability Evaluation Model

## Objective

LossLogic separates source evidence from derived findings and professional liability conclusions.

Liability percentages are not treated as mathematically precise outputs derived from arbitrary point values. Instead, the system identifies supported findings, evaluates the relative contribution of each driver's conduct, and records the rationale for the resulting assessment.

## Evidence Sources

The demonstration claim includes:

- insured statement
- claimant statement
- independent witness statement
- vehicle damage evidence
- scene and accident-mechanics evidence

## Supported Findings

### Insured Conduct

The evidence supports that:

- the insured initiated the lane change
- the claimant vehicle already occupied the destination lane
- the insured observed the claimant before beginning the maneuver
- contact occurred during the insured's lateral movement
- the physical damage pattern is consistent with a sideswipe during the lane change

### Claimant Conduct

The evidence also supports that:

- the claimant was aware of the insured vehicle
- the claimant accelerated during the developing merge
- the claimant intended to maintain position ahead of the insured rather than create additional separation
- an independent witness also observed an apparent increase in claimant speed

## Professional Assessment

The insured's lane-change maneuver is the primary contributing cause of the collision.

The claimant's acceleration during the developing conflict is a secondary contributing factor supporting comparative negligence.

Demonstration liability assessment:

- Insured: 80%
- Claimant: 20%
- Confidence: Moderate-High

## Explainability Requirement

Any generated liability evaluation should identify:

1. the primary contributing conduct
2. any comparative contributing conduct
3. evidence supporting each material finding
4. material conflicts or limitations in the evidence
5. the resulting liability assessment
